# natex v0.3.0 — exhaustive scanning, honest verdicts, calibrated calendar-time inference

This release changes what the automated pipeline tests and how it reports what it
could not test. It follows a review of the paper collection built on v0.2.0, which
found three structural problems: the search plan (heuristic or LLM) decided which
configurations were scanned, five different "could not test" outcomes were reported as
`null`, and calendar-time kinks were gated on HC1 p-values that the project's own
measurements had shown to be oversized.

## Exhaustive role enumeration (discover, survey, `discover --plan`)

- `discover(..., profile=)` enumerates the full role space from the intake profile:
  every binary treatment candidate x every single non-time forcing column (plus the joint
  all-forcing candidate per treatment), and every treatment x profiled panel for did.
  The plan still orders the scan; it no longer bounds it. `searched["role_space"]` lists
  what was enumerated and `searched["excluded"]` lists every column left out with its
  reason (time-like forcing columns, declared outcome columns, treatments that are a
  global deterministic step in a time column).
- Every scanned rdd boundary is estimated against every candidate outcome
  (`summary["effects_by_outcome"]`, via the row-aligned `Dataset.with_outcome`); the
  scan never reads any of them. The survey renders the per-outcome table in the report.
- A search-plan candidate's outcome is treated as a guess: it stays in the forcing space
  and is estimated as an outcome where it is not a role. Only prep-plan-declared outcome
  roles are reserved (`known_outcomes`). Previously a wrong heuristic guess (the first
  numeric column) removed the true running variable from the scan.
- `DiscoverReport.best()` ranks configurations by fitted-null p before raw LLR; LLRs
  are not comparable across forcing dimensions.

## `inconclusive` verdict

The survey's status vocabulary gains `inconclusive`: the family ran but the design could
not be validly tested — a refused placebo test (sc, fewer than 5 usable placebos), a
configuration that could not be scanned, a mechanical rediscovery of a constructed time
step (issue #52), a degenerate kink, density or DEE fit, or a calendar-time kink with
fewer than 19 placebo positions. `null` now means "tested, nothing credible surfaced".
Reports carry a badge for it; the agent skills say it is never a negative finding.

## Calendar-time kinks are gated on the placebo-calibrated p

For a time-like running variable (dates, years, `days_since_<event>`, `wave`, `period`,
`t`), the survey's kink family builds a grid of up to 49 shifted placebo cutoffs and gates
on `placebo_calibrated_p` instead of the nominal HC1 p. The nominal p, the number of
placebos, the floor `1/(n+1)` (`PlaceboKinkGrid.min_attainable_p`) and the placebo
rejection rate (the empirical size of the nominal test at non-event dates) are reported
beside the verdict. Fewer than 19 positions cannot reach alpha and give `inconclusive`.

Checked against the v0.2.0 flagship's own series: the GPQA-Diamond kink at o1-preview
is the most extreme of 49 placebo cutoffs at bandwidths 365–730 days (calibrated p =
0.02, the floor; nominal HC1 rejects at 14% of placebo dates at bandwidth 540), while the
quarterly chip-stock series behind the China difference-in-kinks hosts only 7 evaluable
placebo positions (floor 0.125) and is not gateable at the 5% level on this evidence.

## Documentation and agent skills

- Method cards state the size-not-power reading of placebo rejections, the 19-position
  floor and the role-space coverage; README and AGENTS.md list the six verdicts.
- `natex-write-paper` gains a style contract and a definition of what counts as a
  finding; `natex-survey` and `discover-natural-experiments` ask for the inference
  caveats once, in one paragraph, and for the per-outcome table to lead the rdd summary.
